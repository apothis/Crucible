# Second Skin: treatment (draft for the user, 2026-11-10)

Song: Apotheon, Second Skin, 303.6 s, 76 bpm, one female soprano (Selene). Take used by the
YouTube tab (library 428eda78). Gothic metal: cello and voice in the verses, the band crashing in
on every chorus.

## The story

A woman in a cold mountain village at the edge of a pine forest is turning into a wolf with the
moon. The verses show the evidence the lyric lists, as things happening: mud on her feet, a torn
bed, the bruise, the dog that backs away, the doctor who finds nothing, the candles she no longer
needs, the church bells. The choruses are the thing under her skin pushing up: moonlight, the
forest, the wolf's eyes in the trees. In the middle the village finds the killed sheep and goes
hunting with torches and dogs. In the bridge she walks out to the treeline, folds her nightgown on
a stone and waits in the moonlight. In the final chorus she owns it: the hunters reach the stone and
find only the folded nightgown, while the wolf runs the ridge. The last "it is me" is her face with
the moon in her eyes. At dawn she is asleep in her bed again, mud on her feet. It starts over.

Light across the song: cold blue dawn (verse 1), warm candlelight going dark (verse 2), the moon
growing each chorus (crescent, half, full), orange torchlight against silver in the solo, pure
silver in the bridge, a pale grey dawn at the end.

Payoffs, shown early and again near the end, changed:
- The open window: opens the video and closes it.
- The dog next door: backs away from her in verse 1, howls at the full moon in the final chorus.
- The calendar: days crossed off, then the last one.
- The candles: lit in verse 1, cold in verse 2.
- The folded nightgown on the stone: she leaves it in the bridge, the hunters find it in the
  final chorus.

## Setting and wardrobe (assumptions for the user to confirm)

- **Setting:** a late 1800s mountain village in deep pine forest: stone cottages, a small stone
  chapel with a bell tower, sheep folds, frost. Nothing modern on screen. The lyric's doctor,
  calendar and church fit that period.
- **Her one outfit** (the writer allows one wardrobe for the whole video, with weathering): a
  long ivory linen nightgown, thin and loose, with a ribbon-tied neckline, slipping off one
  shoulder. Bare feet. Hair loose and tangled, pale skin, smudged dark eyes, no jewellery. Mud on
  the hem and her feet is weathering. A torn hem later in the video is also weathering. The bruise
  is on the bare shoulder.
- **The wolf** is a real grey wolf, never a man-wolf creature. No transformation on screen (morphs
  break). She and the wolf never share a frame. Cutting between them tells it.

## Lessons carried over from the last video

- Every scene starts on its own opening picture: hers with her already in place, plates with
  their people. "Starts on its picture" is on for every singer scene.
- A scene at the same place as its neighbour from a new angle gets its own picture. Text alone
  never moves the camera.
- Same place twice in a row is fine. The same angle with someone appearing or vanishing is not.
- Plates without her render on the image-to-video lane from their picture (real speed).
- People in plates each do their own small ordinary business, staggered in time. Never in unison,
  never climbing or jumping.
- Her size is fixed in a shot. Nothing walks toward the lens. A change of scale is a cut.
- A medium close-up or closer while words are sung is always her singing to camera. The first
  line lands on her face.
- Her scenes are one clip (15 s at most). Motion comes from the camera on plates, the cutting,
  wind, light and her gestures.
- Silent shots of her do one deliberate thing (grips the bedpost, throws her head back, lays down
  the nightgown).
- Empty places at most about one scene in five. Show the events the lyric names, with their
  people and animals.
- A moving shot that fails gets 3-4 seeds before it is simplified. A mostly good render gets only
  its called-out shots re-rendered.
- Hand-set mute ranges are available for any scene whose lock hears a word it should not.

## Tests before the full render (box, need a go-ahead, about 20 minutes each)

1. Her running side-on through the pines (her travelling has failed before). If it fails on 3
   seeds, the shot goes to Seedance.
2. The wolf running between the pines (an animal at speed, image-to-video lane).
3. The dog backing into its kennel with her at the gate (an animal reacting near her).
4. The hunters with torches and dogs moving through the forest (a moving group).
5. The doctor beside her (an unreferenced person close to her: identity bleed is untested).
6. Her close-up with gold in her eyes (a change against her face reference).

The other shots are kinds that already passed: singing close-ups, held poses, people plates,
animal plates, the moon and sky plates.

## Order of work

1. The user confirms or changes the story, setting and wardrobe.
2. Claude gives the two reference picture prompts (face, full body) for this wardrobe. The user
   makes them.
3. The vocal stem on the box (about 81 s, needs a go-ahead), for the lyric timing.
4. The writer runs with this treatment as its concept. Claude measures the script against the
   rules (places, empty plates, story people, pop cuts) and fixes through the writer, not by hand.
5. Claude gives the opening picture prompts, in chat. The user makes them.
6. The tests, then the full render.

## The script (2026-11-10)

Written by the writer with this treatment as its concept, on the vocal stem made the same day.
The first run gave 45 scenes but only 16 of them singing, and its outfit sentence predated the
real pictures. The second run, with the descriptions rewritten from the pictures and a firmer
singing count in the concept, is the one in the project:
`second_skin_20261110_102315_rw9_..._rw34_20261110_102510`. Scenes 9, 28 and 34 were re-run
through `rewrite_scene` for sky flags. 9 and 34 cleared. 28 is a false positive: its prose says the
sky is not visible, and only the place description mentions it.

- 46 scenes, 13 places. The biggest place share is 17%, and the longest run in one place is 2 scenes.
- 19 scenes of her singing (at least 3 in every chorus, 2 in each verse) and 8 silent shots of her.
- 13 scenes with story people or animals. Empty places are 20% of scenes.
- No pop cuts and no framing flags.
- The writer dropped her running through the pines: its rules never write her travelling. The
  hunt shows her crouched behind the fallen pine instead.
- After the bridge, she is framed bare-shouldered from the collarbones up, or from behind. The
  nightdress is on the stone. This is a seventh test: her reference pictures show the nightdress.

Tests (scene numbers as the tab shows them): 8 the dog backing away near her, 12 the wolf
running, 16 the doctor beside her, 27 the torch party moving, 33 bare shoulders after the bridge,
